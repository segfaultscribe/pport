Quip dragged his face across the table as the professor's voice drilled into his ear. Lamenting the fact that this period of math class would never end, the only thing holding Quip together was the fact that after school, he could enjoy a warm, refreshing cup of tea before heading home.

Occasionally, Quip looked up and wondered how reading from a screen made one a professor. There was not an ounce of improvisation in the lecture, and it almost felt like listening to an old, rusted, and senile text reader. Half an hour later, Quip was finally released from this torture, and as he packed his bag, he couldn't help but think about that cup of tea that he had been cherishing for the past couple of hours. Without wasting a moment, he rushed to the corridor and picked out each of his friends from their respective classes and convinced them to go for tea. The convincing part was important because getting that tea was not an easy feat.

With this newfound vigor and charged by the golden setting sun, Quip set his journey into motion. The nearest tea shop, which was also the common tea shop among the schoolgoers, was atop a hill. Now, one doesn't need to literally climb this hill to get tea, but the other option was to follow the road, which meant walking a good few kilometers uphill or catching a bus or a ride. The buses were rare, and the rides were expensive. For a bunch of students who had to create a budget for tea, this wasn't an option.
The group, however, preferred climbing the hill. The tea was a main motivator indeed, but the adventure itself was a very attractive feature. The climb was steep and unforgiving, and a few people had to be left behind, but Quip and some of his eager followers, who were now convinced of the magical properties of tea, did not give up. At times they had to rely on a few hanging vines to haul themselves up certain points, and at others a team effort was required. Making it to the top of the hill was also possible via the willpower of a single person, but who wants to do that when you've got a group of friends? Before the final section of the climb, one of the boys in the group slipped and fell. Truly a tragedy. He fell on one of the ridges a few inches below and did not break anything. The group left him there and moved on. The hill had ridges along the sides where you could stop and rest, which considerably distanced mountain climbers due to the very disappointing fact that the climb was too safe. Half an hour later the boys were on the top. Not technically on top of the hill but besides the ridiculously large hairpin on whose side was the very anticipated tea shop.

A shanty that was so old that, ever so often, people would still wonder how it still stood up. All it would take was a small gust of wind or a slight drizzle to remove the shop from its current location and place whatever was left of it on the bottom of the hill. There were researchers from several parts of the town who would approach the tea shop to study the structural integrity of the shack, and the owner would shoo them away while also inquiring why a bunch of communications majors would want to measure structural integrity. The wood was almost pitch black with age and dirt, and the interiors weren't luxurious either.
The crowd was heavy, and the narrow space inside the shop did not make it any easier. Even then, Quip surged forward, his will unwavering. Within a few minutes, he was at the front of the fleet. Finally, it was time for his tea. With the confidence of a zealot, Quip firmly spoke his order, "One tea, please!" Then he proceeded to repeat this three more times as the tea maker simply ignored him the first few times. On his last try, the tea maker noticed him and then, without any sense of acknowledgement, went on with his task of brewing tea.

The tea maker was a short old man who, by the looks of it, never cared about appearances. Either he was old by aging, or he had made so much tea that he started appearing older, but one would definitely never mistake him for being young. Crooked wrinkles lined his forehead, and he wore a pair of broken spectacles that were oddly positioned on his face. He had the expression of a soulless husk of a man who had given up on life yet somehow still clung to it. A tasteless, once plain white shirt covered his body that now took a shade of brown from the exposure to years of dirt. The shirt was tucked in and at the same time not tucked in; no one knew for sure, and none bothered asking. His pants were a different story. It was torn in several places and was adorned with various unrelated designs, sometimes flowers, sometimes shapes, and all in all gave the tea maker the refined look of a mental asylum escapee.

Quip was not happy. He had waited a long time, and still the tea maker gave him no concern at all. Quip took every chance he got to try and catch the attention of the tea maker, yet all he caught was the pity the people around him had. Slowly, Quip was losing his patience, and he merely stared at the tea maker who was brewing tea with such a lavish sense of time that one would believe that the shop had no customers at all! The tea maker did not seem bothered by the pile of people before him and was in no hurry to provide the one thing people came to his shop for. The tea maker occasionally looked at the crowd and handed tea to people in no sense of order. An order placed with the tea maker had no confirmation until he handed the tea to you himself.

Quip thought about leaving a bad review and then decided against it because the shop barely existed in the physical realm; the fact that it would have an online review was beyond rational thinking. Half an hour was the time Quip waited for a tea that took maybe two minutes to brew. Quip continued shouting his order and even started to wave at the tea maker, all to no avail. He witnessed the people who arrived after him leave with their tea, and most of his friends had finished their tea by now. He couldn't put a finger on why the tea maker was specifically ignoring him. Maybe he was, maybe he wasn't, but no one else waited as long as Quip did and still didn't have their order.

A few minutes later, the tea maker made eye contact with Quip. A sense of joy rushed through Quip's veins as he realized he was finally about to get his tea. All this wait wouldn't have been for nothing. The tea maker then proceeded to beat the hot tea into a cup to soften it and then poured it into a glass cup. He then glanced at Quip, brought the glass up, and handed it over to someone behind him. As Quip watched in a mixed sense of horror and anger, the tea maker then proceeded to wipe the stall and simultaneously, while smiling, informed Quip that the tea was over.

Have you ever felt the sense of rationality leave your body so quickly as the line that never must be crossed has been crossed a little too many times? Quip was feeling just that. Pupils dilated and veins popping, anger had consumed Quip. He threw his arms at the tea maker, caught him by the collar, and hauled him across the stall, throwing him into the seating area. The tea maker flew a bit and broke a table or two on landing. Everyone around the counter (there wasn't much since most people had left after finishing their tea) scrambled as the fight broke out in the shop. It wasn't a fight but more so just Quip beating up the tea maker. The former occupants of the table in the seating area now watched in horror as Quip unleashed his vengeance. The tea maker watched in absolute horror and pain as Quip rained down his wrath upon him, and Quip did not even leave an opening where the tea maker could mention the availability of tea for the next day and ask him about his preference. Quip couldn't care about anything else. The tea that had meant everything to him was now gone, and so was Quip. He threw his fists at the tea maker and used every combination of martial arts he had learned by watching TV. An old person sitting right next to the beating sipped his tea eagerly as he enjoyed the show. He even started to keep count of all the teeth that were falling down.

A few friends of Quip who had heard the commotion came running into the shop and pulled Quip away from the tea maker who was now lying on the ground. The boys had to put up a considerable struggle to pull Quip aside. At one point, Quip caught a hollow mug in his hand and threw it at the half-unconscious (or for the optimistic ones: half conscious) tea maker.
After he had been successfully pulled away, Quip quickly regained his composure, went outside and sat on the bench overlooking the distant mountains. It was getting dark, and the sun had disappeared. As Quip took in the final rays of daylight, all he felt was joy, an unusual sense of peace from having beaten up the tea maker and wondered if actually getting his tea on time would have been this fulfilling. The rest of the people in the tea shop had already evacuated themselves. A few minutes later, Quip got up and threw himself at the tea maker again (who was on the ground pretending to be dead), but his friends had anticipated this and successfully neutralized the situation. Quip then agreed not to beat up the tea maker anymore and left the shop promptly with his friends.

On his way back, Quip passed by a busy street famously known for not having tea shops, and at the third intersection Quip heard a call that made him stop dead in his tracks. A girl had called out to him. Quip whipped his head around and found a new rolling shop by the side of the road, and in that moment the entire world started to slow down for him. The shops disappeared, the lights became brighter, and the only shop that remained was the one with the girl. The girl, to Quip, looked almost too beautiful for mortal description, and the word tea resonated around him. He walked towards the shop in disbelief, and as he got closer, he could smell it. The faint smell of dried and crushed tea leaves. As he approached the cart, he saw it. The pot on top was about to overflow with freshly brewed tea. He looked at the face of the girl whom he now thought was an angel and forgot how to speak. "Would you like some tea, sir?" she asked. Quip, who was now starting to tear up, smiled. "One tea, please!"
